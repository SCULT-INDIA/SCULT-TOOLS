The one rejection

Exactly what the research standard is for: an agent found real, adjacent evidence but refused to stretch it into the topic as framed.

How small agencies are actually handling scope creep with AI-assisted contracts

[C105] — score 37/100, evidence: weak

Real, related evidence exists but does not support the specific claim in the topic framing. General scope-creep advice for agencies is well documented (Search Engine Land's 7-step framework), and a genuine, if very early-stage, product category of AI-powered contract-review tools that flag scope-creep risk does exist (several small 2025-2026 Show HN launches: Lexray, SimpleMetrics, Fivani, ContractGhost, ContractShield). However, none of these had more than 1-3 upvotes/near-zero discussion, no case studies or agency testimonials were found, and the flagship scope-creep article found makes zero mention of AI at all. There is no evidence found of an established or documented practice of 'small agencies actually handling scope creep with AI-assisted contracts' — what exists is a handful of unproven, low-traction side projects plus one throwaway comment ('Tell HN: AI programming has turned my scope creep to scope leap', 2 points). Writing a confident 4,500-word article claiming this is how agencies 'actually' operate would outrun the evidence.

What causes scope creep in agency contracts and how do agencies prevent it (non-AI)?

Search Engine Land's 7-step framework: define concrete deliverables, separate strategy from execution pricing, pre-build a change-order process, price for value not hours, set communication boundaries, audit scope quarterly, and say no in writing — with no mention of AI tooling anywhere in the piece.

Do any AI tools exist specifically to catch scope-creep risk in contracts before signing?

A small number of 2025-2026 Show HN launches (Lexray, SimpleMetrics, Fivani, ContractGhost, ContractShield) pitch AI contract analysis that flags scope-related risks (vague acceptance criteria, overly broad IP clauses, milestones that invite scope creep), but each has only 1-3 points and little to no discussion, indicating early/unproven traction rather than established practice.

Is 'AI-assisted contracts' a phrase people actually search or discuss in relation to scope creep?

No direct evidence of this specific phrase or framing was found in forum discussion; the closest was a single low-engagement Hacker News aside ('Tell HN: AI programming has turned my scope creep to scope leap', 2 points/4 comments) referring to AI coding output causing scope expansion, not AI-assisted contracts as a scope-creep defense.
