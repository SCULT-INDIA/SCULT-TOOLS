# What actually changed about Instagram reach for small accounts

Based on Instagram head Adam Mosseri's own public statements as reported across 2025-2026, three specific reach "myths" have been debunked from the platform's own leadership — hashtags don't meaningfully drive discovery, reposting your feed to Stories doesn't boost reach, and sending something via DM doesn't boost reach either. What has replaced those old growth tactics is a heavier emphasis on saves, watch time, shares, and profile clicks, plus a 2024-era structural change that specifically rewards original, smaller creators over content aggregators and repost accounts.

## The three "myths" Mosseri has personally debunked

Most Instagram growth advice repeated online is years out of date, which is part of why this topic generates so much confusion for small accounts. What's more reliable is tracking specific, dated statements from Instagram's own head of product, Adam Mosseri, as reported by outlets covering the platform closely.

**Hashtags.** Coverage from around December 2025 reported Mosseri calling hashtags a "myth" for driving reach, and Instagram subsequently limited hashtag functionality on the platform following that statement (as reported via [The Times of India](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), December 19-20, 2025). This directly contradicts a huge volume of older growth advice ("use 30 hashtags per post") that still circulates in evergreen blog content.

**Reposting to Stories.** Coverage from April 2026 reported Instagram stating that reposting your own feed content to Stories does not boost reach ([WeRSM](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), April 8, 2026) — a tactic many small accounts use specifically because it's low-effort and was assumed to give a post a "second chance" at visibility.

**DMs and shares.** Coverage from May 2026 reported Mosseri stating that DMs specifically do not boost reach ([The News International](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), May 16, 2026) — again cutting against advice that encouraged creators to explicitly ask followers to "send this to a friend."

Two caveats matter here. First, these are all reported statements attributed to Mosseri via secondary news coverage rather than Instagram's own official developer documentation, so treat the exact wording as reported rather than a verbatim primary-source quote. Second, "doesn't boost reach" doesn't necessarily mean "doesn't matter" — a DM share might still drive a direct visit to your profile or a save, which are separately confirmed to matter (see below); it just isn't functioning as a distribution signal the way people assumed.

## The 2024 shift toward original and smaller creators

The foundational structural change behind all of this predates the 2025-2026 statements above. Coverage from April 2024 reported that Instagram updated its algorithm specifically to reward "original content" and penalize content aggregators and repost accounts, with the explicit stated goal of surfacing smaller, original creators more prominently ([TechCrunch and Engadget](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Instagram+algorithm+reach+small+accounts), April 30, 2024).

This matters because it reframes the entire conversation about "the algorithm hurting small accounts." The 2024 change was, if anything, a deliberate structural advantage for original small creators relative to large repost/aggregator accounts — the opposite of the popular narrative that algorithm changes always squeeze out the little guy. Coverage specifically addressing how small creators are affected differently than established influencers by more recent changes reinforces that the relationship between account size and algorithmic treatment is not simply "bigger accounts always win" ([Cyprus Mail](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Instagram+algorithm+reach+small+accounts), May 11, 2026).

There's also a real, documented technical bug worth separating from the myth-busting above: Engadget reported that Instagram fixed a genuine technical bug that had been causing posting multiple Stories in a short window to tank reach ([Engadget](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), September 12, 2025). This is a useful example of the nuance in this topic — "posting too many Stories hurts reach" was, for a period, not a myth at all but an actual (and later corrected) bug, which is a different category of claim than the hashtag/repost/DM statements above that were characterized as myths from the start.

## Engagement vs. reach: why they're being talked about differently now

Coverage from May 2026 reported Mosseri stating that engagement rates now matter more than raw reach for how a post performs ([Storyboard18](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), May 27, 2026). This is a meaningful distinction for small accounts specifically, because it changes what "success" should look like on a dashboard.

Reach measures how many distinct accounts saw your content at least once. Engagement rate measures how many of the people who saw it actually did something — liked, commented, saved, shared. A small account with modest reach but a high engagement rate is, per this framing, now treated more favorably by the distribution system than a large account with big reach numbers but a low proportion of viewers actually engaging. Independently verifiable benchmark data on typical engagement-rate ranges supports this being a meaningfully different game by account size: one 2025-2026 industry analysis reports accounts under 10,000 followers seeing like-to-follower ratios of roughly 8-10%, compared to roughly 3-4% for accounts over 100,000 followers ([DataSlayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/instagram-algorithm-2025-complete-guide-for-marketers)) — meaning smaller accounts structurally tend toward higher engagement rates even before any algorithmic preference is applied, simply because their audience is smaller and more self-selected.

The engagement-rate benchmark above (8-10% vs. 3-4% like-to-follower ratio) is separately sourced and should not be confused with these unverified reach-percentage figures.

## What actually seems to move the needle in 2025-2026 coverage

Pulling together the reported statements above with the broader wave of 2025-2026 Instagram-algorithm coverage, a consistent picture emerges of which specific behaviors are being discussed as genuinely mattering for distribution, separate from the ones debunked above:

- **Saves** — repeatedly named across 2025-2026 coverage as one of the strongest individual signals, on the logic that a save indicates content valuable enough to want to return to, a stronger intent signal than a quick like.
- **Watch time on Reels** — specifically named in coverage of the platform's ranking signals, reflecting Instagram's continued heavy weighting toward video content performance.
- **Profile clicks** — treated as a signal that content is interesting enough to make someone want to learn more about the account behind it, not just the individual post.
- **"Sends per reach"** — a specific named metric that received a dedicated breakdown from Influencer Marketing Hub, treated as something creators should track directly rather than a vague "shares are good" heuristic ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), March 3, 2026).
- **Using Instagram's own tools (e.g., the Edits app)** — Social Media Today reported Mosseri stating that using Instagram's own editing app for Reels boosts reach, as part of a broader push dispelling engagement myths ([Social Media Today](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), August 10, 2025) — a pattern consistent with platforms generally favoring content produced with their own native tools over content produced elsewhere and simply uploaded.
- **Interpreting your own Insights data correctly** — Social Samosa reported Mosseri sharing detail specifically on how creators should interpret Instagram's built-in performance insights, suggesting the platform is trying to shift creators away from vanity metrics (likes, follower count) toward the ones it says actually drive distribution ([Social Samosa](https://news.google.com/rss/search?q=Adam+Mosseri+Instagram+reach), May 27, 2026).
